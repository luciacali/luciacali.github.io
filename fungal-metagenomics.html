Six years later: Ectomycorrhizal fungal communities recovering after the end of long-term nitrogen and sulfur addition in a mixed-species temperate North American forest

Project Overview

This project examined the recovery of ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungal communities following the conclusion of a 26-year nitrogen and sulfur addition experiment at the Bear Brook Watershed in Maine. Soil samples collected at the end of the experiment in 2016 were compared with samples collected six years later in 2022 to investigate whether fungal community diversity and composition recovered after experimental nutrient additions ceased. Using ITS2 metabarcoding and Illumina platform next-generation sequencing, the study characterized ECM fungal communities across treated and reference watersheds. The results showed that six years after the experiment ended, ECM fungal species richness in the previously treated watershed had increased to levels comparable to the reference watershed. The nitrophobic genus Cortinarius showed a particularly strong increase, while overall community composition remained distinct from the reference watershed. These findings provide evidence that ECM fungal communities can recover, at least partially, following prolonged nitrogen enrichment.

My Role

I led the field work and contributed to the molecular and computational components of the project. I secured undergraduate research funding through the Center for Undergraduate Research and used the award to support two undergraduate lab members who assisted with field sampling and DNA extractions. I performed DNA extractions and PCR amplification of fungal ITS2 sequences from environmental soil samples and prepared samples for Illumina-based metabarcoding. I also contributed to bioinformatic processing and analysis of the resulting sequencing data, including sequence quality control, filtering, taxonomic assignment, phylogenetic analysis, and characterization of ectomycorrhizal fungal communities. I worked with the resulting datasets to organize, analyze, and visualize fungal community data, including ordination analyses to examine patterns in community composition and contributing to the statistical and ecological interpretation of the sequencing results. This work gave me experience spanning the full workflow from environmental DNA extraction and molecular amplification through high-throughput sequencing, bioinformatic processing, and downstream data analysis and visualization.
