Molecular characterization of spawning-associated RNA expression in Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) using RT-qPCR

Project Overview

This ongoing project investigates the use of environmental DNA (eDNA) and environmental RNA (eRNA) to improve molecular monitoring of Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) spawning activity. Traditional fisheries monitoring methods can be expensive, difficult to replicate across large marine environments, and provide limited information about the biological activity of fish populations. While eDNA can provide evidence of species presence, it does not necessarily indicate what those organisms are doing. Environmental RNA offers a complementary approach because RNA is less persistent in the environment and may provide a more temporally relevant signal of biological activity. Our approach combines an eDNA assay to confirm the presence of Atlantic cod with eRNA assays targeting genes associated with spawning activity. The project includes controlled mesocosm experiments and field sampling at the Whaleback Closure to evaluate whether eRNA signals vary with spawning activity and whether an integrated eDNA–eRNA framework can be applied to marine field samples. Candidate eRNA biomarkers were identified through analysis of RNA-sequencing data and are being evaluated through RT-qPCR assay development and testing.

My Role

I contributed to field sampling and the molecular development of the project, including nucleic acid extraction and testing of both eDNA and eRNA assays. I developed a qPCR assay targeting the mitochondrial cytochrome b (CytB) gene to confirm the presence of Atlantic cod in environmental samples. I also developed and evaluated RT-qPCR assays targeting several candidate loci associated with spawning activity. To identify potential eRNA biomarkers, I reviewed RNA-sequencing data and identified CCNA1 as a candidate target for further assay development. I subsequently designed and evaluated primers and probes for RT-qPCR detection of this transcript. The resulting assay demonstrated strong quantitative performance, with a standard-curve R² of 0.998. This work has involved integrating field sampling, nucleic acid extraction, RNA-sequencing data analysis, and molecular assay development to investigate the potential of eRNA as a tool for detecting fish spawning activity.
