## The Paper's Message

The review listed above pulls together the latest research surrounding the role of autophagy in neurobiology, and how loss-of-function can lead to neurodegenerative and neurodevelopmental diseases including Alzheimers, Huntingtons, autism, and ADHD. 

"WIPI proteins regulate autophagy via mTORC1–AMPK signaling, while mutations in these proteins can cause intellectual disability, neurodevelopmental abnormalities and neurodegeneration."(Kim et al., 2026, p.11)

Briefly, autophagy can be thought of as the cells waste-management and recycling system. Discovered by Belgian biochemist and Nobel laureate Christian de Duve in 1963, The word arises from Greek roots "auto-" and "-phagy" which translates to "self" and "to eat" respectively. Unlike nearly every other cell in the body, neurons are extremely long lived &mdash; often surviving for years and decades. They are also spatially unique, with long tendril-like extension (axons & dendrites) with most of their organelles centralized in the soma. Survivability therefore is extremely dependent on robust waste transport and clearance.

Autophagy has traditionally been thought of as a stress response to cellular starvation. Perhaps the central message from Kim et al. (2026) is that autophagy also performs essential housekeeping functions under normal conditions. In other words, this system not only deploys emergency cleanup crews, but also operates routine waste management services.
